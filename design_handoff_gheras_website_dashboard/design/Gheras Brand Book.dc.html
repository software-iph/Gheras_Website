One season, many generations.

Gheras means seedlings — things that have been planted. The whole brand is about planting with intent and waiting for value to grow. The logo already holds the story in two plants.

غراس هي الشتلات، وما غُرس في الأرض. العلامة كلها قائمة على الغرس بنيّة، وانتظار القيمة حتى تنمو. والشعار يحمل القصة في نبتتين.

Wheat · Todayالقمح · اليوم

The harvest of today.

Wheat grows and is harvested within a single season. It is food security, now — what we deliver this year.

القمح يُزرع ويُحصد في موسم واحد. هو الأمن الغذائي اليوم، وما نسلّمه هذا العام.

Olive · Tomorrowالزيتون · الغد

The legacy of tomorrow.

An olive tree takes years to bear fruit and can live for centuries. It is patience, legacy and Libyan heritage.

شجرة الزيتون تحتاج سنوات لتثمر، وتعيش قروناً. هي الصبر والإرث وتراث ليبيا.

PHOTO — aerial, centre-pivot fields in the desert, golden hour
The second insight — the circle

Green circles in the sand.

Seen from the air, Libya’s irrigated desert farms — in Kufra, for example — are perfect green circles, drawn by centre-pivot irrigation. The logo is a circle too. The circle becomes the signature shape of GHERAS: modern irrigation, precision, and life made out of desert. It joins heritage and technology in one form.

من الجو، تبدو مزارع الصحراء الليبية المروية — في الكفرة مثلاً — دوائرَ خضراء كاملة، يرسمها الري المحوري. والشعار دائرة أيضاً. لذلك تصبح الدائرة الشكلَ المميِّز لغراس: ري حديث، ودقة، وحياة تخرج من الصحراء.

05Logo — master fileالشعار

The original logo, at production resolution.

SOURCE FILE — 744 × 816 px
ENHANCED — vector-traced edges, original colour · sharp at any size

Using the master now

  1. The client’s logo is used exactly as drawn. Nothing about its shapes, colours or wordmarks has changed.
  2. Every edge — plants, GHERAS and غراس — was traced to vector, so outlines stay sharp at any size. The gradients inside are the client’s original colour, cleaned and sharpened.
  3. The descriptor line is reset as live type (IBM Plex Sans Arabic and Manrope), since the source text was too small to recover.
  4. On Deep Olive, use the reversed version: the wordmarks switch to Linen, and the plants stay in colour.
  5. One-colour versions are pure vector silhouettes, ready for foil, stamps, embroidery and laser cutting.

Known limits of a raster logo

  1. The fine wheat hairs in the source were too faint to recover and are omitted.
  2. Interior shading is still raster. For very large formats (signage over about 2 m), export the one-colour vector or commission the full redraw.
  3. Keep the master at or above the minimum sizes. Below 32 px, the emblem reads as a shape only.

Refinement — proposed, on hold

  1. Both plants redrawn as flat vector shapes. No gradients.
  2. Fewer grains and leaves, all drawn from one shared curve.
  3. English and Arabic wordmarks balanced in weight.
  4. An emblem that works without the words.
PROPOSAL — NOT IN USE UNTIL APPROVED

Vector refinement — construction

SPINESTwo mirrored cubic curves inside circle R. They cross at the base — the sweep where today meets tomorrow.
ONE PRIMITIVEGrains and leaves share one lens shape. Grains sit at ±18° to the stalk and taper 25% to the tip; leaves sit at ±40° and taper 30%.
WORDMARKSGHERAS in Fraunces SemiBold, +4% tracking. غـراس keeps the logo’s kashida, set inside r = 0.6R.
Production note. The Arabic wordmark here uses Noto Naskh Arabic as a stand-in. For the master artwork, a type designer should redraw the client’s original Arabic letterforms to this weight and alignment.

Rules for Arabic and English together

Mixed lines
Latin inside Arabic uses the Latin font of the same role, size-matched.
تعمل GHERAS على [000] ha
No letter-spacing on Arabic
Arabic letters connect. Wide tracking is for English capitals only.
No fake styles
Real weights only. Arabic has no italics — emphasise with weight or colour, in both languages.
Kashida is reserved
The long stroke in غـراس is for the logo and large display moments. Never to justify body text.
Ragged, never justified
Arabic body right-aligned, English left-aligned.
Side by side
Arabic sits on the right or on top. Both look equally important.
